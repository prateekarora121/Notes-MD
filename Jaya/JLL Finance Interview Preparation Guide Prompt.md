## Candidate profile and purpose

The candidate is an experienced finance professional with 10 years of R2R experience. The guide should help them confidently connect their R2R background to a broader, client-facing finance role involving financial control, month-end close, client billing and receivables, account reporting, commercial management, stakeholder relationships, and team leadership.

Do not assume the candidate has performed responsibilities or used tools that are not stated in the profile. Where a strong answer needs the candidate's real figures, systems, industry, team size, or achievements, use clear placeholders such as `[insert actual close timeline]` rather than inventing facts. Provide sample answers that are realistic and adaptable, not claims that the candidate must repeat verbatim.

Explain how R2R experience transfers to the role, and clearly distinguish direct R2R ownership from adjacent experience in client accounting, accounts receivable, billing, commercial finance, or account management. Do not present adjacent experience as established candidate experience.

## Teaching and answer style

Write an interview preparation guide, not a textbook, quiz, or generic list of questions. Explain what each question is assessing, how to structure a strong answer, and which evidence or examples the candidate should prepare.

Use clear, professional English suitable for a senior finance interview. Keep answers concise enough to speak naturally, while giving enough detail to demonstrate sound judgment. For behavioral and leadership questions, use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) or a similarly clear structure. Include realistic sample answers tailored to a 10-year R2R professional, with placeholders wherever personal facts are required.

Include practical, real-world-style examples throughout the guide so the candidate can see how finance concepts play out in day-to-day work. Examples may use clearly labeled illustrative scenario details (including sample amounts or timelines where useful), but must not be presented as the candidate's actual achievements or as facts about JLL or a specific client. For personal interview answers, use placeholders and instruct the candidate to replace them with a truthful example from their own experience. Where the profile does not confirm direct experience, demonstrate an honest transferable-skills answer rather than inventing one.

For technical answers:

* Explain the process, key controls, risks, reconciliations, ownership, and escalation points.
* Distinguish accounting principles and common practice from JLL-specific procedures that are not supplied.
* Do not invent JLL systems, policies, client data, performance targets, or internal processes. Advise the candidate to confirm the actual tools and controls during the interview.
* Use accurate finance terminology and explain any important distinction, especially where R2R connects with billing, accounts receivable, budgeting, and commercial management.
* Discuss confidentiality, auditability, segregation of duties, approval evidence, and policy compliance where relevant.

## 3. Technical and process interview questions

Create likely interview questions with answer frameworks and sample responses for each of the following areas.

### Financial control and compliance

Cover account-level financial controls, policy adherence, approval limits, segregation of duties, supporting documentation, audit trails, risk assessment, control exceptions, remediation, and escalation. Include questions about identifying a control weakness, handling a late or unsupported journal, and balancing close deadlines with control requirements.

### Month-end close and R2R

Cover close calendars, cut-off, accruals, prepayments where relevant, journal preparation and review, balance-sheet reconciliations, intercompany items where relevant, variance analysis, substantiation, close dependencies, late inputs, and post-close review. Include a practical walkthrough of how to investigate an unexpected balance or material variance.

Explain how to present the candidate's R2R experience in terms of accuracy, timeliness, ownership, risk reduction, and decision-useful reporting, without making up metrics.

Include at least one concrete illustrative close example, such as how a finance professional investigates an unexpected expense variance by tracing it to source transactions, checking cut-off and accrual support, validating the journal and approval trail, quantifying the impact, and communicating the conclusion and any corrective action. Clearly label any sample amounts or dates as illustrative.

### Client invoicing, accounts receivable, and cash flow

Cover invoice accuracy and completeness, billing data and approvals, billing schedules, contract or purchase-order alignment, dispute handling, AR ledger integrity, aging analysis, collection prioritization, unapplied cash awareness, and coordination with client/account teams. Explain how R2R knowledge supports reliable billing and receivables without implying that R2R and O2C are the same function.

Include scenario questions about a disputed invoice, overdue balance, billing error, incomplete backup, and a recurring collection delay.

For at least one scenario, show a realistic end-to-end example: validate the invoice against approved billing support and relevant contract or purchase-order information, identify the dispute owner, document the issue, agree a resolution timeline, update the appropriate stakeholders, and monitor the receivable through resolution. Make clear that the candidate should only claim direct billing or collections ownership if it reflects their actual experience.

### Financial reporting, glide path, and budget variance

Explain how to prepare a clear account-level financial story: actuals versus budget/forecast, period and year-to-date views, trend, drivers, risks, actions, and owner. Define “glide path” in the context of tracking performance toward a financial target or plan, while noting that the exact JLL/client definition and measures should be confirmed with the interviewer.

Include questions about unfavorable variances, changing assumptions, incomplete data, explaining results to non-finance stakeholders, and correcting a misleading or unclear report.

Add a worked illustrative example of explaining a variance in plain language, moving from the reported difference to its underlying driver, financial impact, forecast implication, and recommended action. Use clearly labeled sample figures only; do not imply that they came from JLL or the candidate.

### Planning, forecasting, savings, and commercial analysis

Cover forecast assumptions, run rates, committed versus discretionary costs, sensitivity, risks and opportunities, savings baselines, initiative ownership, validation of realized savings, avoidance of double counting, benefit timing, and business-case review. Explain why a claimed saving needs a defensible baseline and evidence.

Include questions about a forecast that appears unrealistic, a savings claim unsupported by evidence, and a commercial decision with incomplete information.

### Contracts and client-account commercial understanding

Cover reviewing relevant commercial terms, billing triggers, service scope, assumptions, approval responsibilities, change control, and how finance escalates uncertainty. Do not provide legal advice; show how finance should identify issues and involve the appropriate commercial, operational, or legal owner.

### Process and performance improvement

Cover trend analysis, root-cause analysis, recurring errors, manual effort, aging close items, service-level or timeliness trends where applicable, corrective actions, ownership, and measuring whether a change worked. Include a structured example answer with placeholders rather than invented results.
