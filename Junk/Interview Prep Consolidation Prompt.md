# Prompt: Consolidate Interview Preparation Notes

You are editing the interview-preparation notes in this workspace. Consolidate the following three source files into one clear, well-structured Markdown document:

1. `Interview Prep Master Q&A - Hinglish.md`
2. `NET Full Stack Interview Q&A Sheet.md`
3. `new interview prep.md`

## Important: work in two phases

### Phase 1 — show a sample and wait

Read and compare all three source files. Identify a topic that appears in more than one file and prepare a representative sample of how that topic would look in the consolidated document.

The sample must:

- Combine the related questions and answers under one topic instead of repeating them in separate source-based sections.
- Preserve useful distinct details, examples, trade-offs, pitfalls, and follow-up questions from the source material.
- Show the proposed heading hierarchy and question-and-answer format.
- Be substantial enough to demonstrate how overlap and unique information will be handled.

Return only:

1. A brief proposed top-level outline for the consolidated document.
2. The representative sample section.
3. A short note identifying which source topics were combined and any important content intentionally kept distinct.

**Then stop. Do not create, edit, or overwrite the final consolidated document until the user approves the sample. Do not modify the source files.**

### Phase 2 — consolidate after explicit approval

After the user approves the sample, create `Interview Prep Master - Consolidated.md` in the workspace by consolidating all three source files.

## Organization requirements

- Organize the final document by subject, not by source filename. Keep closely related questions, answers, examples, and follow-ups together in the same topic or subsection.
- Use a clear progression from fundamentals to advanced concepts and practical scenarios.
- Add a table of contents with working internal links and use consistent, descriptive heading levels.
- Group duplicates and near-duplicates. Create one primary question where appropriate, and combine complementary answers into a complete, readable answer.
- Keep genuinely different perspectives, depth levels, or interview variants as clearly labeled follow-ups or alternate angles under the same topic; do not flatten meaningful distinctions.
- Do not discard unique questions, implementation details, code examples, tables, caveats, or useful interview advice merely because another source covers the same broad topic.
- Keep question-and-answer pairs together. Do not place questions in one section and their answers elsewhere.
- Preserve technical accuracy and the intent of the original material. Do not invent personal experience, metrics, company-specific claims, or citations. Keep placeholders such as `[apna example]` when they are intentional.
- Preserve the source language of each answer (English or Hinglish) unless the user asks for translation. Use consistent headings and formatting without silently rewriting Hinglish answers into English or vice versa.
- Remove only redundant introductions, repeated explanations, or duplicate content that adds no distinct value. Keep source-specific context when it is important to understand an answer.
- Normalize obvious formatting inconsistencies, spelling errors, and broken Markdown without changing technical meaning.
- Include a concise source note in the final document identifying the three files used. Do not copy entire source documents into appendices as a substitute for consolidation.
- Do not modify or delete any source file. Do not change unrelated workspace files.

## Quality checks before delivering Phase 2

- Confirm every source file was reviewed and every substantive question was either included or deliberately merged into a related question.
- Check that related questions and answers are colocated and the same topic is not needlessly split across distant sections.
- Check for accidental omissions, duplicate passages, broken code fences, malformed tables, and incorrect table-of-contents links.
- Report the output path and briefly summarize the consolidation. Do not claim checks that were not performed.
